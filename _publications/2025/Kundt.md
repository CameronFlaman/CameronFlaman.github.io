title:          "Serving Kundt: An Investigation Into the Acoustic Absorptivity of Charcuterie" 
date:           2025-04-29 00:01:00 +0800
selected:       true
abstract: >-
  Many a scientific theory has been discussed over a board of charcuterie, but has the scientific theory of a board of charcuterie ever been discussed? This report employs a modified Kundt’s tube across a wide range of frequencies to produce standing waves. The node and anti-node intensity of these standing waves allow for the determination of the acoustic absorbance for the charcuterie items of interest: meat, cheese, bread, and board. While this experiment yielded results high in both precision and repeatability, there was a consistent positive bias in absorbance values as a result of limitations in testing apparatus and methodology. Despite numerous improvements made as a means to improve accuracy, the setup did not yield results in sufficient accordance with industry standards. However, the Kundt’s tube employed in this experiment proved an effective method of comparing relative absorbances to a high degree of precision. The obtained values of absorbance relative to one another were in alignment with predicted relationships. Porous and of low density, bread acted as an effective sound absorber, with a greater absorbance than the non-porous and dense homogenized meat and cheddar cheese tested. Plywood, being relatively moderate in both density and porosity, was found to exhibit an absorbance value in between the other materials. While the materials investigated in this paper may appear as humorous, these novel measurements can be applied as a non-invasive means of quality control, as well as in other diverse uses in science and engineering.
