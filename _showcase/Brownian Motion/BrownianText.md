    <p class="card-text"> For my physics measurements laboratory course, my team constructed this setup to macroscopically test the colliding-ball analogies for microscopic Brownian and diffusive motion models. Consisting of an Arduino-controlled actuating frame, small bearing balls were launched on a level, low-friction surface, colliding with each other and a 3D-printed central "puck". A computer vision blob-detection model was applied to this central puck to observe its displacement per sample time, and this data was fit to theoretical Brownian motion and diffusive models. 
    </p> 
    <p> The resultant plots demonstrated a strong correlation between the displacement of the central puck over large time intervals and these theoretical predictions. While unfiltered displacements aligned strongly with complex subdiffusive models, excluding anomalously large displacements (caused by ball density, collision elasticity, and other physical defects) the model approached the distinct displacement histogram shape of 2D Brownian motion. See the setup in action <a href="https://youtu.be/U35jqvFsYhA" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">here</a>.
