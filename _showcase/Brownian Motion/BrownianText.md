    <p class="card-text">
      For my advanced physics measurements laboratory course, my team constructed this setup to macroscopically test the colliding-ball analogies for microscopic brownian and diffusive motion models. Consisting of an Arduino-controlled actuating frame, small bearing balls were launched on a level, low-fiction surface, colliding with each-other and a 3D printed central "puck". A computer vision blob-detection model was applied to this central puck to observe its displacements per sample time, and this data was fit to the theoretical Brownian motion and diffusive models.
    </p>
    <p>
      The resultant plots demonstrated a strong correlation between the displacement of The Center puck over large-time intervals, and these theoretical predictions. While unfiltered, displacements aligned strongly with complex subdiffusive models, when excluding anomalously-large displacements (caused by ball-density, collision elasticity and other physical defects) the model approached the distinct displacement histogram shape of 2D Brownian motion.
    </p>
